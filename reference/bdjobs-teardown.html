What Bdjobs does, and which parts Marwa should take

A feature-by-feature pass over Bdjobs — home, search, job cards, the posting page and the whole signed-in jobseeker panel — judged against what an overseas recruitment agency actually needs. Three verdicts: copy it, adapt it, leave it.

Reviewed bdjobs.com and mybdjobs.bdjobs.com Target apply.marwaoverseas.com Signed-in panel viewed on Ruman's own account
The frame

They are a marketplace. Marwa is the agency.

This decides every borrowing call below, so it is worth stating plainly before the list.

And the number that settles it

Their recruiter product sells access to a database of 6.2 million CVs. Broken down by category, that database is this:

648,237Accounting / Finance
568,125Banking
170,262Call centre
2,648Carpenter
593Caregiver / Nanny
315Boiler operator

On the largest job site in Bangladesh, in the trades Marwa actually recruits for, the whole national database holds a few hundred people. Bdjobs is a white-collar product with a blue-collar veneer — and they know it, which is why blue-collar postings are the one thing they give away free.

Bdjobs sells listings and access. Their product ends when a candidate hits Apply Now — what happens next is between the jobseeker and the employer, and Bdjobs never finds out. That is why their paid features are all about visibility: matching percentage, profile views, employer-interested signals, points and rewards.

Marwa's product starts where theirs ends. The licence, the employer relationship, the medical, the visa, the deployment — none of that is something a listings marketplace can do, and all of it is what a worker going to Japan or the Gulf is actually buying.

So the rule is: match Bdjobs on everything a candidate does before applying, because that is the experience they are used to and anything clumsier reads as amateur. Beat them on everything after, because there they have nothing. Never compete on volume — 5 next to 5,776 is a comparison Marwa loses, so the portal should never invite it.

One honest limit on this review: I read the jobseeker panel while Ruman was signed in. I did not open anything behind a payment, and the employer-side product I only saw from its public pages.

Area B

Search and filtering

Collapsible facet groups in a left rail
Quick Filter, Category/Industry, Location, Posted/Deadline, Other Filters — each folds.
Copy
Done. Built on <details> so it needs no JavaScript, and groups holding an active choice open themselves server-side.
Counts on every option
Each facet value shows how many jobs sit behind it.
Copy
Done, and done better. Bdjobs shows global counts; ours are computed against the filters already applied, so the number is exactly what you get. Options that would return nothing are not offered at all.
Type-to-narrow inside a long facet group
A search box inside the Category group filters the option list.
Copy
Done. Appears only on groups with eight or more options, and is inserted by script — so with JavaScript off there is no box at all, rather than a box that looks like a search and does nothing.
Salary, Age and Experience range sliders
Three drag-handles under Quick Filter.
Adapt
Sliders are the wrong control here and the data is not there yet. Overseas pay is quoted in four currencies and usually "negotiable at offer stage", so a salary slider would filter on a number Marwa cannot stand behind. Age is worth having as a plain band, because destination countries impose real age limits. Experience belongs as "No experience needed" — their Fresher checkbox — which needs one new field on the job post.
Jobs per page, result total, and visible pagination
"5,776 Jobs | 22,489+ Vacancies", page numbers, 20/50 per page.
Copy
Done, minus the vacancy count — Marwa's job posts do not record how many positions each vacancy covers. That is one field away if you want it, and it is a genuinely useful number for a recruiter.
Favourite Search (saved searches)
Save a filter combination and return to it; lives under Personalization.
Adapt
Worth having as an alert, not a bookmark. With five open jobs nobody needs to save a search — they need to be told when a sixth appears in their trade. "Email me when a welding job opens" is the version that fits Marwa.
Area C

The job card

The three things you flagged are all here.

Company logo on every card
Logo sits right of the title; cards without one fall back to nothing.
Adapt
The reason half their cards have no logo is that a logo costs ৳3,900. Displaying the company logo is a paid upsell on their Premium tier — it is not that employers decline, it is that it is behind a paywall. Marwa charges nobody, so showing a mark on every card costs nothing and reads as more established than the site it was borrowed from. Rule: employer logo where the employer has agreed in writing, otherwise a sector mark (介護, 建設, ビルクリーニング) — so every card has a visual anchor either way, and no card is ever blank.
Hover expands the card
Hovering reveals the education and requirements block inline, and the card border highlights.
Copy
Genuinely good and not yet built. It lets someone triage a list without losing their place, which is exactly what a results page is for. Two conditions: it must expand without moving its neighbours — reserve the space or overlay it — and it must have a tap equivalent, because most of this audience is on a phone where hover does not exist.
Icon-and-label meta row
Location pin, briefcase for experience, calendar for the deadline.
Copy
Done. Scans far faster than words joined by separators, and survives translation into Bangla without re-flowing.
Deadline on the card, in red
Closing date shown without opening the posting.
Copy
Done. It is the single fact that decides whether a posting is worth opening. Ours additionally turns red inside seven days.
Featured band above the results
Paid placements in a two-column grid, marked with a star.
Adapt
Built, but it must never become an ad slot. On a marketplace, featured means paid. On an agency's own portal it should mean urgent — the intake closing first, the employer interviewing next week. Same component, honest meaning.
Area D

The job posting page

Sticky action bar — deadline, Apply Now, Save, Share, Print
Follows you down the page as you read.
Copy
Overseas postings are long. Putting Apply at the bottom of two thousand words of requirements loses people. Share matters more here than it does for Bdjobs — this audience passes vacancies to each other on WhatsApp, and that is Marwa's cheapest channel. Print is worth keeping too: candidates take printouts to upazila offices and to families.
Section tabs — All / Requirements / Responsibilities / Company Information
Sticky tab strip that jumps within the posting.
Copy
Forces postings into a consistent shape, which is the real benefit — it stops staff pasting one undifferentiated block of text. For Marwa the tabs should be Requirements · The work · Pay and contract · What Marwa handles, that last one being the part no marketplace can write.
Facts strip — Vacancy, Experience, Location, Published, Salary
A fixed row of labelled values above the body text.
Copy
Same reason. Marwa's version should carry contract length, destination, language requirement and the exams required — for Japan, naming the specific test (介護技能評価試験, JFT-Basic) rather than "language skills required".
Matching Percentage gauge
Scores your profile against the posting. Paid.
Adapt
Keep the idea, invert the purpose, never charge for it. As a paid teaser it is a sales device. As a free, specific checklist — "You have the care certificate; you still need JFT-Basic" — it is the most useful thing on the page, and it reduces the hopeless applications Marwa staff currently have to screen out by hand.
Report this Job / Company, with a fraud warning in Bangla
Standing warning that no employer should ever ask for money, plus a complaint phone number and email.
Copy
The most important single thing on their site for Marwa's audience, and the easiest to overlook. Overseas recruitment fraud in Bangladesh is endemic and it is committed by people impersonating licensed agencies. A standing, prominent "Marwa never charges a jobseeker, and here is how to check our licence and report anyone claiming otherwise" protects candidates and protects the licence. Build this one first.
Follow Employer
Subscribe to a company's future postings.
Skip
Wrong relationship. A candidate's relationship is with Marwa, not with a Japanese care home they have never heard of. Follow-a-trade or follow-a-destination is the same mechanic pointed somewhere useful.
Area E

The jobseeker panel

This is the part you asked about, and it is where Bdjobs is strongest. Their full structure, then what Marwa's should be.

What they have

A persistent left rail carrying an avatar with a completeness ring, the name, and seven collapsible groups. The content area then has its own two levels: five section tabs, each with its own sub-tabs, and every panel inside is an accordion card with its own Edit and Collapse. Three levels of navigation, and it does not feel heavy, because the rail never moves.

Their left rail

  • Dashboard
  • Manage Profile — Bdjobs Profile, Video CV, Customized CV, Email CV
  • Invitations — Video Interview, General Interview, Online Test
  • My Activities — Applied Jobs, Emailed CV, Shortlisted Jobs, Following Employer, Transaction Overview
  • Points & Rewards — My Points, How do I earn Points
  • Employer Activities — Employer Viewed CV, Employer Message, Employer Interested
  • Personalization — Favourite Search, Promo Codes
  • Account Settings · Help Video · Sign Out

Their profile sections

  • Personal Information — Personal Details, Address Details, Career and Application, Preferred Area, Relevant Information, Disability Information
  • Education/Training — Education, Training, Professional Certification
  • Employment — Experience, Employment History (retired army)
  • Other Information — Skill, Extracurricular, Language Proficiency, Link Account, Reference
  • Accomplishment — Portfolio, Publication, Award/Honor, Project, Other
Two details worth stealing outright. A Profile Visibility toggle (public or hidden) and an Immediately Available toggle, both sitting at the top of the profile. For Marwa the second one is gold: "ready to travel now" versus "available from March" is a question staff otherwise have to ask every candidate by phone.

What Marwa's should be

Their structure is built for white-collar office hiring in Bangladesh. About half of it is dead weight for a man applying to a construction job in Japan, and the half that is missing is the half that decides whether he can actually go.

Rail

  • Overview — applications and their stage
  • My profile
  • My documents
  • My CV
  • Job alerts — new
  • Browse jobs · Sign out

Profile sections

  • About me — name, date of birth, district, contact, emergency contact, height and weight
  • My trade — sector, trade, years, no-experience flag
  • Work history — built
  • Languages — Japanese level, English, exams passed and dates
  • Travel readiness — passport, previous overseas work, available from
  • Training and certificates — TTC, BMET, trade tests
  • References

Dropped, and why

  • Religion, marital status — not Marwa's to ask
  • Disability Information — sensitive; only if a destination legally requires it, and then at offer stage
  • Retired army history — a Bangladesh-specific hiring convention
  • Portfolio, Publication, Award — white-collar furniture
  • Points & Rewards, Promo Codes — gamification for a marketplace
  • Link Account — social profiles do not place a welder

Height, weight and blood group are not white-collar furniture — they are on Bdjobs' form and they are genuinely needed for overseas placement: uniform and PPE sizing, and the medical that every destination requires. Keep those.

Area F

CV tools

You asked for CV creation like theirs. Here is exactly what theirs is.

"We've built a resume based on your profile" — with completeness ring, last-updated date, Download and View
The structured profile is rendered into a CV automatically. No writing required.
Copy
Marwa already does this — the generated CV exists and is linked from the account. What is missing is the framing: theirs announces itself at the top of the profile with the date and a download, so the candidate knows it exists. Ours hides behind a link. That is a presentation fix, not a build.
Customized CV
Choose which sections appear and generate a tailored version.
Adapt
One template per destination, chosen by Marwa, not by the candidate. A Japanese employer expects a different document from a Gulf employer, and the candidate has no way of knowing that — asking him to choose pushes expertise onto the person with the least of it. Marwa picks the format from the destination of the job he is applying to.
Video CV
Record and attach a short video to the profile; employers can request one.
Adapt
The strongest idea on their site for Japan specifically. A thirty-second clip of a candidate greeting an employer in Japanese demonstrates something no written level ever will, and Japanese employers do ask. But it is the most expensive thing on this page: storage, upload on poor connections, and a review step so nothing unsuitable reaches an employer. Phase two, and worth it.
Email CV
Send your CV to an employer address from inside the panel.
Skip
Actively wrong for a licensed agency. Marwa submits candidates to employers; a candidate mailing himself round behind the agency undercuts the relationship and the licence. Staff-side "send this shortlist to the employer" is the feature that belongs here instead.
Download Profile
PDF of the generated CV.
Copy
Ours prints through the browser rather than generating a PDF server-side — deliberate, because the cheap PHP PDF libraries mangle Bangla and Japanese. The browser's own Save as PDF handles both correctly. Worth a visible Download button that says so.

Done, and this is where Marwa wins outright. Bdjobs can show that you applied. Marwa shows the stage — screened, shortlisted, interview, medical, visa, deployed — because Marwa is the one moving it. Theirs is a receipt; ours is a tracker.
Employer Viewed CV / Employer Interested / Employer Message

Adapt

Adapt
Low value at five open jobs, rising as the board grows. Worth building when there are enough vacancies that someone cannot hold them in their head — not before.

Area I

Talent Search

Their candidate-search screen is the closest thing they have to Marwa's staff side.

Filter chips — Age, Experience, Industry, Edu Institute, Edu Subject, Salary
Six axes across the top of a keyword search.
Adapt
Three of their six axes are education. For a construction job in Japan, which university someone attended is irrelevant and which exams they have passed is decisive. Marwa's axes are trade, district, years, passport status, Japanese level, available-from and destination already worked.
Saved Filters / Shortlisted CVs / Purchase List
Three standing lists above the results.
Adapt
Shortlisted CVs is the one that matters — it is the shortlist pack already on Marwa's "not built yet" list, and seeing it in use confirms the shape: a persistent named list a recruiter builds across sessions, then sends. Purchase List is their billing, and has no equivalent.
Category grid with CV counts
Every occupation with how many CVs sit behind it.
Copy
Marwa's staff need exactly this view of their own pool — how many welders with passports are on file, how many carers hold JFT-Basic. It is the screen that answers "can we accept this demand letter?" before the answer is promised to an employer.
What to build

Order of work

Ranked by what it does for a candidate, not by what is quickest.

Fraud warning and licence verification

A standing block on every posting and in the footer: Marwa never charges a jobseeker, here is the licence number, here is how to verify it, here is how to report anyone using Marwa's name to ask for money. Cheapest thing on this list and the one that protects real people.

Profile restructure

The seven sections above, with the completeness ring and Download CV promoted to the top of the page the way theirs is. Adds travel readiness, exams and available-from; drops religion, marital status and the white-collar sections.

Job posting page rebuild

Sticky action bar with WhatsApp share and print, the four section tabs, the facts strip naming the actual exams, and the free eligibility checklist in place of their paid matching gauge.

Interview scheduling fields

Date, time and place on the interview stage. Three fields; removes a round of phone calls per candidate.

Card expand, and logos

Expand-in-place on hover with a tap equivalent. Logos need your answer first on whether employers may be named publicly — sector marks are the fallback.

Job alerts

Email when a vacancy opens in a candidate's trade or destination. Their Favourite Search, pointed at a board small enough that waiting is the normal state.

Video CV

Phase two. Highest value for Japan, highest cost in storage, upload and moderation. Worth doing properly or not at all.

Nothing in this review proposes publishing a figure Marwa cannot stand behind. Where a Bdjobs feature depends on data Marwa does not hold — vacancy counts, salary bands, employer branding — the note says so rather than suggesting a placeholder.
